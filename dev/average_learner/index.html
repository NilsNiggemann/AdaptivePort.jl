AverageLearner

AverageLearner estimates the mean of a noisy function. The function takes an integer seed and returns one real sample; the seed must be its only source of randomness. The learner asks for the seeds $0, 1, 2, \dots$ and stops when the standard error of the mean is within the tolerance.

using AdaptivePort, Random

noisy(seed) = 1.5 + randn(Random.Xoshiro(seed))
learner = AverageLearner(noisy; atol=0.05)
BlockingRunner(learner)            # stops by itself, no tol needed
result(learner)                    # (mean, std, n)

examples/average_learner.jl is the runnable script.

Algorithm

With $n$ samples, sample standard deviation $s$ and mean $\bar y$, the standard error is $s / \sqrt{n}$. The loss is that error divided by atol, or by rtol times $\lvert \bar y \rvert$, whichever is larger; it is Inf below min_npoints samples. So the loss is at most 1 exactly when the mean is within tolerance, and done(learner) (without tol) tests loss <= 1. ask hands out the smallest seeds that are neither told nor pending, so told seeds are never repeated and abandoned seeds can be asked again after remove_unfinished!. done(learner; tol) compares the loss to tol instead.

Limits

Float64 only. The mean and variance come from running sums of $y$ and $y^2$ (as upstream), which loses precision when the mean is much larger than the standard deviation. The standard error assumes independent samples. Differences to upstream: Deviations.

API

AdaptivePort.AverageLearner — Type
AverageLearner(f; atol = nothing, rtol = nothing, min_npoints = 2)

Learn the mean of a noisy function. f(seed) returns one real sample, and the integer seed is its only source of randomness. The points the learner asks for are the seeds 0, 1, 2, .... Port of upstream's AverageLearner.

The loss is the standard error of the mean divided by atol, or divided by rtol * abs(mean), whichever is larger. So the loss is at most 1 when the error is within tolerance, and done(learner) means exactly that. It is Inf below min_npoints samples.

Keyword Arguments

  • atol: desired absolute tolerance of the mean.
  • rtol: desired relative tolerance of the mean. At least one of atol and rtol is required.
  • min_npoints: samples to take before the loss is finite; at least 2, because the standard deviation needs two.
